# Enhanced grounding of OCBF: research synthesis

**Subtitle:** Bitemporal object-centric histories, heterogeneous evidence, and probabilistic process reasoning  
**Date:** 9 September 2026  
**Status:** Research synthesis and proposed formal grounding; not an adopted design or an empirical validation  
**Companion:** [Lean proposal](ocbf-grounding-proposal.md)  
**Scope:** Theory, modeling, mathematical interpretation, and high-level evaluation. No implementation specification, delivery roadmap, or deployment recommendation.

## Abstract

Mammut's available data combine telemetry, derived states, production commands, reconstructed operations, plans, and attribution outputs. Their principal difficulties are uncertain interpretation, correlated provenance, repeated and revised reports, incomplete observation, and inconsistent temporal and identity conventions. A fixed object-centric semantic skeleton provides a useful common language, but neither an OCEL representation nor a collection of assertion marginals resolves these difficulties by itself. This synthesis proposes a joint belief model over admissible object-centric histories, conditioned on explicit observation mechanisms. It separates factory time from knowledge time, source reliability from applicability and dependence, descriptive reconstruction from normative conformance, and evidence influence from causal attribution. Conventional factor graphs, mixed graphical models, temporal PGMs, uncertain event logs, temporal event knowledge graphs, and robust Bayesian evidence supply complementary foundations. The resulting model supports reusable process queries while making its dependence on grounding, provenance, priors, and approximation explicit.

**Keywords:** OCBF; elastocel; OCEL; OCED; DOCEL; temporal event knowledge graphs; bitemporal data; factor graphs; mixed graphical models; belief propagation; uncertain conformance; evidence provenance; robust Bayesian inference.

## 1. Purpose, requirements, and research method

### 1.1 Intended contribution

The proposed grounding is:

> OCBF is a bitemporal probabilistic model over semantically admissible object-centric histories, informed by revisable observations with explicit applicability, reliability, dependence, and temporal scope.

This is a synthesis of established methods and a proposed integration for this context. No novelty priority, calibration guarantee, general tractability result, or proof of factory-scale effectiveness is claimed.

The user's governing clarification is that the **semantic skeleton remains fixed**. Adaptability concerns signals, their interpretations and assertion dynamics. It does not require discovering new object types, changing the ontology, or learning the factory's meanings from arbitrary fields. A fixed vocabulary nevertheless permits new candidate instances and changing states of existing instances.

The delivery preference is executive/business insight with high deliverability, meaningful heterogeneous evidence, manually configurable baseline trust, and uncertainty-aware conformance and attribution. No delivery date, staffing budget, hosting target, or accepted screen specification has been supplied. Proposed modeling choices below should not be read as accepted implementation requirements.

### 1.2 Evidence categories

The document distinguishes four kinds of statement:

| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Observed archive property | A property of stored records or derived outputs inspected in the session. |
| Source-semantic finding | Behavior established by inspecting source code or a declared data definition. |
| Literature result | A result or modeling distinction supported by an identified paper or standard. |
| Proposed construction | A mathematical or conceptual choice recommended here, with its assumptions stated. |

A stored record's existence does not authenticate the physical event it purports to describe. Likewise, a source code path does not prove that every historical record was generated by that path or configuration.

### 1.3 Method and limitations of the review

This is a structured, critical research synthesis, not a systematic review or an exhaustive survey. Literature was selected to address concrete modeling questions arising from the local evaluation. Searches, citation-neighbor expansion, and targeted full-text passage checks used Firecrawl's research index; official specifications, author-hosted papers, and publisher records supplied additional primary material.

Load-bearing checks concerned uncertain-evidence semantics, mixed-model compatibility, BP's variational interpretation, temporal event-data representations, duration assumptions, and object-centric conformance. Bibliographic metadata were checked where needed. An arXiv upload date is not assumed to be the original publication date; references use persistent identifiers when publication chronology is immaterial.

The empirical basis is the **9 September 2026 inspection record**, supplemented by source reads during the theoretical discussion. Creating these documents did not re-extract the factory, rerun its analytical layers, fit OCBF, or perform physical ground-truth authentication. The evidence register records paths and revision boundaries.

## 2. Empirical grounding and evidence limits

### 2.1 Existing assets and their roles

The intended Mammut source is the mamnufac checkout, not the separate mamco checkout. Its catalogue declares Job, Operation, Station, Operator, Team, Shift, Buffer, ProductType, and additional types with incomplete coverage. The catalogue describes an Operation as a routing-step instance, currently reconstructed as a per-chassis station visit. Entry/exit timestamps become operation start/completion, and operator co-presence supplies proposed participation links [E1].

Elastocel already translates a domain catalogue into an OCBF Schema, ConstraintRegister, candidate Universe, and derivation report. Its structure work is an existing asset. An operational, heterogeneous-evidence interpretation layer is not established merely by this translation [E2].

The data-extract folder contains retained Kafka envelopes, typed silver layers, and selected gold exports. These preserve useful observation and reporting information. They do not constitute a complete lifetime history of the factory [E3].

These findings are documented in [E3–E5]. The distinct number of topics, source identifiers, payloads, or rows must not be substituted for the number of independent observation mechanisms.

### 2.4 Reconstruction, conformance, and attribution

Earlier stored ontology outputs contained 1,401 reconstructed visits, including 1,215 closed and 186 open visits. Of the closed visits, 929 lasted under one minute. This motivates scrutiny of visit-to-operation abstraction; it does not establish that those visits were false [E6].

The stored attribution result classified three waits, totaling 3,860.17 minutes, as downstream_busy. Recorded downstream-occupancy overlap was 158.04 minutes. The inspected rule assigned the whole wait when any overlap existed. Thus the classification is not measured causal allocation of the full duration. Forty-one waits totaling 4,049.75 minutes were unclassified; a stored cause_confidence of 1 did not resolve the causal meaning [E6].

Stored standard-time rows used a reconstructed p20 frontier, and populated independent routing/staffing references were limited. Discovery fitness on the same reconstructed history is not independent conformance validation. An inter-visit gap also does not establish that the job was ready for productive work throughout that gap.

### 2.5 Physical provenance boundary

The active producer source was checked on GitHub at commit de6f301fa1d9bcaead2b7971038b04aaadb4b07e, rather than relying on the stale local FactoryMonitor checkout. A successful deployment record exists for that revision. Neither establishes continuous runtime configuration or per-record historical revision [E4].

No simulation markers were found in the searched retained payloads. However, inspected simulation code can write shared production history directly, and relevant history rows lack a verified physical-origin attestation. An empty simulation topic therefore does not certify the archive. Conversely, the existence of simulation code does not prove contamination of the inspected records.

Known defaults and simulated values cannot enter as observations of actual execution. Unverified physical provenance remains an explicit applicability assumption or unresolved limitation; arbitrary low trust does not authenticate it.

## 3. Terminology and literature map

### 3.1 Object-centric representations

OCED denotes the broader domain of object-centric event data. OCEL is a concrete log representation; a resolved log is one realization of event occurrences, object references, and values. OCEL 2.0 supports qualified E2O/O2O relationships and timestamped object-attribute changes. Its O2O relationships are atemporal, and its events have occurrence timestamps [R1].

DOCEL, Data-aware OCEL, explicitly associates a dynamic attribute value with the object and the event that changed it. That addresses change attribution as well as temporal storage [R2]. Temporal Event Knowledge Graphs, tEKGs, represent persistent entities and temporal snapshots, making the properties applicable at an event more directly accessible [R3]. Neither representation independently supplies uncertainty calibration.

Recent gOCED research grounds objects, events, properties, and dynamic relationships in gUFO, addressing ambiguities in object-centric event-data models [R4]. Its distinctions are useful for evaluating Mammut's mappings; adopting a replacement ontology is outside this proposal.

Object-centric process executions generalize cases to graphs of interacting objects and events. Variants are equivalence classes under declared projections or graph equivalence, rather than necessarily activity strings [R5].

### 3.2 Four graphs with different meanings

| Graph | Nodes and relationships | What an edge does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Object graph | Factory objects and their relations or shared participation. | Statistical independence or causation. |
| Event graph | Events with ordering, directly-follows, or synchronization structure. | A causal mechanism merely from temporal order. |
| Provenance graph | Records, transformations, interpretations, and derivations. | Independence between records with different identifiers. |
| Factor graph | Random variables and factors defining a joint measure. | A factory relationship or intervention effect without a semantic model. |

The OCEL schema supplies type restrictions and possible factor scopes. It does **not** imply a statistical factorization. Relations that can be represented separately can remain probabilistically dependent.

### 3.3 Research families and their contribution

PGM denotes a probabilistic graphical model. Here, MGM denotes a mixed graphical model with different variable domains; it is a kind of PGM. Markov networks and their factor-graph representations are also covered, without treating an undirected statistical edge as a causal arrow.

| Family | Contribution | Principal boundary |
|---|---|---|
| OCEL, DOCEL, tEKG, gOCED [R1–R4] | Object/event/attribute/relationship semantics and temporal representation. | Representation is distinct from evidence and probability. |
| Object-centric behavioral constraints and artifacts [R6, R29] | Data-aware lifecycle obligations. | Normative requirements are not occurrence evidence. |
| Uncertain logs and alignments [R7–R9] | Realizations, uncertain conformance, synchronization, fragment boundaries. | Bounds and probabilities require different assumptions. |
| Factor graphs and variational inference [R10, R11] | Joint factorization and message passing. | Convergence and local consistency are not general accuracy guarantees. |
| Mixed and latent graphical models [R12–R14] | Coupling heterogeneous random-variable domains. | Compatibility, normalizability, and distributional assumptions matter. |
| Credal and uncertain evidence [R18, R19] | Partial likelihood knowledge and robust posterior envelopes. | A confidence score is not automatically a likelihood. |
| Temporal data and temporal PGMs [R22–R25] | Knowledge/valid time, irregular observations, duration dynamics. | An observed time series does not establish a Markov model. |
| Probabilistic databases and provenance [R26, R27] | Possible-world queries and derivation explanations. | Provenance is distinct from probability and causality. |

## 4. Semantic worlds and candidate grounding

### 4.1 Notation

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $\mathcal S$ | Fixed semantic skeleton. |
| $\mathcal U_\kappa$ | Candidate universe used at knowledge time $\kappa$. |
| $L$, $\ell$ | Random object-centric history and one realization. |
| $\Omega_{\mathcal S,\mathcal U}$ | Admissible history space. |
| $t$, $\tau_e$, $\kappa$ | Factory time, event occurrence time, and knowledge time. |
| $X_a(t)$ | Existing semantic assertion $a$ evaluated at factory time $t$, where applicable. |
| $D_\kappa$ | Available evidence interpreted with explicit lifecycle semantics. |
| $K$, $H$ | Auxiliary association variables and observation-system states. |
| $g$, $c_\kappa$ | Evidence group and appropriately conditioned context. |
| $\theta$, $\Theta$ | Complete model configuration and a declared family of configurations. |
| $P_0$, $P_{\kappa,\theta}$ | Prior and evidence-conditioned posterior. |
| $\mathcal N$ | Normative reference, including its applicable scope/version. |
| $q$, $\Xi_\rho$ | Query and execution-extraction function with scope policy $\rho$. |

Sums and integrals below are with respect to appropriate counting, continuous, or trajectory measures. Undefinedness through semantic inapplicability is distinct from lack of evidence.

### 4.2 Definitions

**Definition 1 — Skeleton.** Let

$$
\mathcal S=(\mathcal T_O,\mathcal T_E,\mathcal Q,\mathcal A,\operatorname{sig},\mathcal H)
\tag{1}
$$

specify object/event types, qualifiers, attribute domains, legal signatures, and definitional constraints. Object identity and type remain clamped for the present grounding. Uncertain association of a report with a known object is represented in the observation model, not by silently changing the object's type.

**Definition 2 — History.** In compact OCEL-like notation,

$$
L=(E,O,\operatorname{etype},\operatorname{otype},
\operatorname{time},E2O,O2O,\operatorname{eattr},\operatorname{oattr}).
\tag{2}
$$

This is shorthand, not a verbatim replacement for the complete OCEL specification. Candidate existence variables select the events and objects present in a realization. Event labels, times, qualified links, and applicable attribute histories may be uncertain.

If an existing relationship's intended semantics is stateful, a temporal interpretation $\rho_q(o,o',t)$ may enrich its history. Such an enrichment is not claimed to be losslessly expressible as a plain OCEL 2.0 O2O table. A static relationship does not become physically dynamic merely because belief about it changes.

**Definition 3 — Admissibility.**

$$
\Omega_{\mathcal S,\mathcal U}
=\{\ell:\ell\models\mathcal H,\ \ell\text{ is supported by }\mathcal U\}.
\tag{3}
$$

For example,

$$
R^{EO}_{e,q,o}=1
\Rightarrow V^E_e=V^O_o=1,
\qquad
R^{EO}_{e,q,o}=1
\Rightarrow(\operatorname{etype}(e),q,\operatorname{otype}(o))
\in\operatorname{sig}.
\tag{4}
$$

Here $V^E_e,V^O_o$ are existence indicators and $R^{EO}_{e,q,o}$ is the qualified E2O relation indicator.

An absent event's label, timestamp, and attributes use a unique inapplicable interpretation or are eliminated conditionally. Arbitrarily many meaningless values must not give absence extra probability mass.

### 4.3 Three roles for constraints

Definitional constraints determine admissibility. Descriptive expectations are defeasible beliefs. Normative references define what should happen. Existing defaults for cardinality or lifecycle order must not be promoted to definitions without checking their meaning.

A proper descriptive prior can be written

$$
P_{0,\theta}(d\ell)
=Z_{0,\theta}^{-1}
\mathbf 1_{\Omega}(\ell)\,
\exp\!\left[-\sum_j\beta_j d_j(\ell)\right]\,
P_{\mathrm{base},\theta}(d\ell),
\tag{5}
$$

with descriptive penalties $d_j\geq0$, weights $\beta_j\geq0$, a proper base measure, and $0<Z_{0,\theta}<\infty$. Penalty scales and units must be declared. A normalized prior is not necessarily a substantively suitable prior.

For conformance, the primary reconstruction should not receive an occurrence likelihood merely because $\mathcal N$ requires an occurrence. Reference-assisted reconstruction can be a separate, explicitly conditional analysis; it is not independent validation against that reference.

### 4.4 Grounding is part of the model

An unrepresented event is outside support, not posterior-proven absent. A timestamp-derived lifespan is not automatically the object's actual lifetime. Candidate generation, type clamping, pruning, and source-dependent selection can remove the truth before inference begins.

All probabilities are conditional on the stated universe and coverage assumptions. When candidates are derived from the same evidence subsequently evaluated, their generation must not add a second independent evidential contribution. Truncating a broader population to those candidates requires a coverage/selection qualification.

Normalization must also prevent accidental preference for representations with more legal assignments. For example, construct a normalized conditional distribution over link configurations given event type, rather than relying on an unnormalized count of configurations as an implicit type prior.

Mammut requires separate cardinality scopes for Job-subject and Operation-subject links. Start/completion order must be grounded to the same Operation instance. A broad all-starts-before-all-completions rule on a Job is inappropriate for repeated operations.

## 5. Evidence interpretation and the joint model

### 5.1 Reports, claims, and observation mechanisms

A report records what a source emitted. A claim is an interpreted assertion about the factory. A likelihood describes how that report would arise under alternative truths. These are three different objects.

A conceptual report carries an identifier, source/version, raw payload, observation/publication/ingestion clocks, lineage, episode identity or alternatives, revision relationship, interpretation version, and origin status. Some fields may be unknown. The interpretation contract declares which existing assertions the report can address, its units and censoring, and its relationship to other evidence.

Unknown field semantics remain uninterpreted. Defaults can be declared assumptions or scenario inputs, but not measurements. A source can supply a multidimensional observation about several assertions; splitting it into independent unary claims requires justification.

Association variables $K$ range over compatible known objects/events and an unresolved outcome. The unresolved outcome is an observation-model state, not a fictitious factory object. Evidence used to infer $K$ must not also be inserted as an independent copy of the resulting assignment.

### 5.2 Evidence lifecycle

$D_\kappa$ is the evidence state after applying declared lifecycle semantics to information available by $\kappa$. It is not simply every received row, nor automatically the newest row per source.

| Evidence action | Meaning |
|---|---|
| New observation | Additional information, with dependence modeled. |
| Retransmission | Another delivery of existing information. |
| Revision | Correction or refinement of an earlier report. |
| Reinterpretation | Changed mapping of retained evidence to assertions. |
| Retraction | Withdrawal of support; not automatically a negative assertion. |
| Interval closure | Additional endpoint information about an existing episode. |
| Coverage loss/reacquisition | Changed observation conditions and continuity. |

Independent contradictory observations remain evidence of disagreement. Supersession requires a justified relation between reports. Revision history may itself be informative about source quality if explicitly modeled; it must not become accidental extra votes.

### 5.3 Proposed joint probability model

Let $H$ represent shared observation conditions, biases, upstream errors, or source modes. Evidence groups contain related observations or episode histories. Conditional independence between groups is a declared assumption **after** accounting for $L,K,H$ and context.

Define

$$
\Lambda_{\kappa,\theta}(\ell)=
\int
\left[
\prod_{g\in\mathcal G_\kappa}
p_\theta(D_{\kappa,g}\mid\ell,k,h,c_\kappa)
\right]
p_\theta(dk,dh\mid\ell,c_\kappa).
\tag{6}
$$

The posterior is

$$
\boxed{
P_{\kappa,\theta}(d\ell)=
\frac{\Lambda_{\kappa,\theta}(\ell)\,
P_{0,\theta}(d\ell)}
{\int_{\Omega}\Lambda_{\kappa,\theta}(\ell')\,
P_{0,\theta}(d\ell')}
}
\tag{7}
$$

for a finite, positive denominator. Any prior dependence on the declared conditioning context is implicit in $P_{0,\theta}$. Random informative context belongs in the evidence or joint model; conditioning on context must not silently discard its selection mechanism.

Equation (7) is the central proposed grounding. It is conventional Bayesian conditioning over a relational possible-world space [R10, R26]. The contribution here is the explicit semantic/evidential separation and its application to Mammut. The equation does not specify a particular inference algorithm.

A zero normalizer identifies an incompatibility among support, constraints, and evidence assumptions. It is not a reason to silently relax semantic meaning or fabricate a posterior.

## 6. Trust, dependence, and observation coverage

### 6.1 Manual baseline trust

For a binary assertion $X$, within an applicable observation opportunity and specified context,

$$
\alpha_s=P(Y_s=1\mid X=1),\qquad
f_s=P(Y_s=1\mid X=0).
\tag{8}
$$

Sensitivity $\alpha_s$ and false-positive rate $f_s$ determine the likelihood ratios

$$
\mathrm{LR}_s^+=\alpha_s/f_s,\qquad
\mathrm{LR}_s^-=(1-\alpha_s)/(1-f_s),
\tag{9}
$$

with endpoint cases handled explicitly. A scalar accuracy hides asymmetry and prevalence. Observer-error models provide a classical precedent [R20].

Trust is indexed by source family, assertion kind, version, and relevant context. A detector can be good at place presence and weak at execution completion. Manually set parameters are subjective model assumptions until calibrated.

Parameter uncertainty may be expressed by a declared set $\Theta$ or a justified parameter prior. For example, $\alpha_s\sim\operatorname{Beta}(\nu_s m_s,\nu_s(1-m_s))$ separates a stated mean $0<m_s<1$ from confidence $\nu_s>0$ in that mean. This is a possible elicitation device, not a fitted result or mandatory modeling component.

Applicability, reliability, and independence are separate. Low reliability cannot repair an unsupported semantic mapping or a wrong identity association.

### 6.2 Posterior scores and virtual evidence

Suppose an upstream system provides $r_s(x)=P_s(X=x\mid y)$ using prior $\pi_s(x)>0$. Under compatible observation semantics, its likelihood is proportional to

$$
L_s(x)\propto r_s(x)/\pi_s(x).
\tag{10}
$$

This follows from Bayes' rule. Reusing the posterior as a likelihood generally reuses its prior. Any shared input evidence must still be accounted for.

Virtual evidence supplies likelihood ratios; Jeffrey-style soft evidence specifies a revised marginal under additional assumptions about preserved conditionals. These are not interchangeable interpretations of any supplied probability vector [R18, R19]. SEE's constant placeholder establishes neither.

### 6.3 Dependence and provenance

For a shared upstream observation $Z$,

$$
p(y_1,y_2\mid L)
=\int p(y_1,y_2\mid z,L)\,p(dz\mid L).
\tag{11}
$$

This generally differs from $p(y_1\mid L)p(y_2\mid L)$. Normalized telemetry, live state, and persisted production history can be successive transformations in this chain.

Parameter pooling and shared error are different: sources can share a reliability distribution while their individual reports remain dependent for another reason. A source-family label or an effective-sample-size diagnostic does not itself alter a likelihood.

Exact deterministic copies carry no new information given their originals. Complementary derived views can reveal information absent from another retained view. Unknown lineage calls for declared dependence alternatives, not automatic independence or arbitrary maximum-confidence fusion.

### 6.4 Silence and missingness

For a defined reporting opportunity, let $M_s$ indicate whether a report is observed:

$$
p(M_s,Y_s\mid X,c)
=p(M_s\mid X,c)\,p(Y_s\mid M_s,X,c).
\tag{12}
$$

The likelihood of silence is $P(M_s=0\mid X=x,c)$. It is neutral only when constant in $x$ under the complete relevant conditioning model. A positive-only detector can make silence informative; an unavailable sensor or a missing retained partition does not automatically supply a negative measurement.

Opportunity, sensor availability, selection, delivery, and retention should not be collapsed. Ignoring missingness requires conditions, as established in missing-data theory [R21]. The opportunistic/complete/selective labels require corresponding probability semantics; they cannot be inferred from payload absence alone.

## 7. Time and dynamic variants

### 7.1 Bitemporal assertions

The central temporal query is

$$
P_{\kappa,\theta}(X_a(t)=x),
\tag{13}
$$

which distinguishes factory validity time $t$ from knowledge time $\kappa$ [R22]. Changes in $X_a(t)$ describe possible physical evolution; changes in $P_\kappa(X_a(t))$ describe epistemic evolution.

A late corrected tag binding may revise a historical association. A late completion report may revise an earlier endpoint. Neither means that the physical transition occurred at ingestion time.

Object-attribute changes, temporary membership, and static Operation-Job links need their own temporal interpretations. Current assignments cannot be projected backward without evidence or an explicit persistence assumption. Likewise, event participation through E2O concerns the referenced event; it is not automatically an indefinitely valid state relation.

### 7.2 Points, intervals, and censoring

An Operation interval is determined by matched lifecycle events:

$$
I_u(L)=[\tau_{\mathrm{start}(u)},\tau_{\mathrm{complete}(u)}).
\tag{14}
$$

If the endpoint is unobserved, the history model distinguishes unknown completion from observed survival through a horizon. Right-censoring is not an infinite physical duration. A gap between visits is not automatically productive waiting or readiness.

A reported interval can mean a trusted bound, a coarsened measurement, or alternatives of unknown probability. Only when the reporting mechanism licenses it can a noisy interval measurement use a likelihood such as $F_\epsilon(u-\tau)-F_\epsilon(l-\tau)$. A bound does not imply a uniform timestamp distribution [R8].

### 7.3 Snapshot, trajectory, and prospective models

Let $S_t=\Phi_t(L)$ be a derived object-centric state. A snapshot belief $P_\kappa(S_t)$ supports time-local questions. A trajectory belief $P_\kappa(L_{[t_0,t_1]})$ preserves history needed for ordering, conformance, and exposure. Prospective beliefs require a transition model beyond retrospective evidence interpretation.

Filtering updates present-state beliefs, smoothing revises earlier-state beliefs, prediction concerns future states, and retrospective reconstruction concerns a selected history. Belief revision can change the observation interpretation underlying all four.

### 7.4 Dynamic PGM variants

| Variant | Contribution | Assumption or limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed-grid dynamic Bayesian network | Repeated transition/observation structure. | A time grid and temporal dependency structure are chosen. |
| Irregular-time Bayesian network | Uneven temporal spacing and delay-aware dependencies [R23]. | Transition behavior must account for elapsed time. |
| Continuous-time Bayesian network | Coupled finite-state processes without a fixed grid [R24, R25]. | Basic CTBN semantics uses Markov dynamics and asynchronous local jumps. |
| Semi-Markov or clock-augmented model | Duration-dependent residence and transition behavior [R24]. | Duration families and conditioning assumptions must be justified. |
| Dynamic mixed factor model | Coupled discrete, continuous, and count quantities across time. | The joint distribution must remain coherent and normalizable. |

One possible transition factor is $p(S_{t_i},H_{t_i}\mid S_{t_{i-1}},H_{t_{i-1}},\Delta t_i)$. This is a modeling assumption, not a property inferred merely from timestamped records.

For a duration $D$ under fixed context $c$, conditional on genuinely remaining in the state through age $a$,

$$
P(D>a+h\mid D>a,c)
=\frac{\overline F(a+h\mid c)}{\overline F(a\mid c)}.
\tag{15}
$$

Basic exponential residence is memoryless; clock-augmented models permit age dependence [R24]. If context changes during residence, an appropriate path-dependent survival model is needed.

An object-centric event can synchronize several objects. Basic asynchronous CTBN transitions must not silently replace such joint-event semantics. Joint factors may be required, and participation in one event does not mean every participating object's state changed.

### 7.5 Staleness is question-dependent

Under a transition model, current-state prediction propagates an earlier belief through elapsed time. This can reduce the relevance of an old location observation to current location. It does not erase the observation's historical evidential value.

Tracking loss changes observation continuity and possibly the uncertainty of state propagation. It does not directly assert physical departure, work completion, or a universal decay of all historical assertions.

## 8. Mixed distributions and inference

### 8.1 Heterogeneous sources versus mixed random variables

Heterogeneous files and messages do not imply that every payload field needs its own random variable. The variable domains follow the fixed semantic assertions and the observation model. A mixture of source formats is an interpretation problem; a mixture of Boolean, categorical, continuous, count, and temporal variables is a distributional problem.

| Quantity | Possible mathematical domain | Necessary qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Event occurrence or qualified relation | Boolean | Absence of a report is not the false value. |
| Candidate report-to-object association | Finite alternatives, including unresolved association | Alternatives require compatible typed identities and prior weights. |
| Activity or state | Unordered categorical | Numeric category codes do not imply distances or order. |
| Timestamp, duration, or measurement | Continuous, possibly constrained or censored | Gaussianity and exact observation are separate assumptions. |
| Participants or event count | Nonnegative integer | Default zeros and ones are not count observations. |
| Observation mode or shared error | Discrete or continuous latent variable | Its role is an observation mechanism, not a new factory ontology. |

Mixed graphical models provide ways to couple such domains. However, valid full conditional distributions cannot be chosen independently without checking that they are compatible with a proper joint distribution [R12]. A pseudolikelihood is a product of conditional terms used for estimation; it must not silently replace a joint observation likelihood.

Latent Gaussian and copula approaches can separate some marginal distributions from dependence [R13]. An unordered category is not a continuous measurement transformed monotonically, and a latent conditional-dependence graph is not automatically an observed-variable independence graph. Neither graph is causal merely because it was estimated.

For OCBF, typed higher-order factors can express one-of-many associations, same-Operation lifecycle constraints, and shared observation mechanisms. These relationships can be lost by reducing the model to pairwise correlation discovery. Conditional Gaussian or mixture representations remain useful where justified, but collapsing competing station or episode interpretations to a mean can destroy their semantic meaning.

### 8.2 Factor graphs and belief propagation

Let $z$ collect a finite grounding of history and auxiliary variables, with posterior density or mass proportional to $\prod_{f\in F}\psi_f(z_f)$. Sum-product messages take the conventional form

$$
\begin{aligned}
m_{i\to f}(z_i)
&\propto \prod_{h\in N(i)\setminus\{f\}}m_{h\to i}(z_i),\\
m_{f\to i}(z_i)
&\propto \int \psi_f(z_f)
\prod_{j\in N(f)\setminus\{i\}}m_{j\to f}(z_j)\,
d\mu_{f\setminus i}(z_{f\setminus i}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
$$

The integral includes summation for discrete variables. Priors and likelihoods are factors; messages are computational objects, not new observations. Sum-product is exact on a tree under the usual well-definedness conditions. On loopy graphs, ordinary BP is generally approximate and may fail to converge [R10].

The variational interpretation is useful. For locally consistent normalized beliefs $b_f,b_i$, the Bethe free energy is

$$
F_{\mathrm B}(b)
=\sum_f\mathbb E_{b_f}[-\log\psi_f]
-\sum_f H(b_f)
+\sum_i(d_i-1)H(b_i),
\tag{17}
$$

where $d_i=|N(i)|$. Under appropriate regularity conditions, BP fixed points correspond to stationary points of a constrained Bethe problem [R11]. This does not make every fixed point a global optimum. On a loopy graph, local consistency is weaker than membership in the set of marginals realizable by a common joint distribution. Differential entropy and support require care for continuous or deterministic factors.

Three statements therefore differ: the messages converged; the approximate beliefs satisfy local constraints; the reported probability is accurate and calibrated. None should be substituted for another.

### 8.3 Relevant modern inference directions

| Direction | Potential contribution to this model | Limit of the contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Cluster or generalized message passing | Retain important local dependencies and synchronized event structure. | Larger regions increase cost; approximation remains model-dependent. |
| Constrained variational or convexified free-energy methods [R11] | Better-characterized optimization of selected approximations. | Optimizing an approximation is distinct from exact posterior recovery. |
| Expectation propagation, including trajectory messages [R25] | Approximate continuous or temporal messages by tractable families. | Moment matching may suppress alternatives; CTBN inference remains difficult. |
| MCMC over coherent worlds [R26] | Estimate joint process-query distributions. | Mixing, rare events, hard constraints, and Monte Carlo error need assessment. |
| Probabilistic circuits [R16, R17] | Efficient exact evaluation of supported queries on an appropriate circuit. | Smoothness/decomposability and related structural properties determine tractability; arbitrary inference or compilation is not free. |
| Hinge-loss Markov random fields / probabilistic soft logic [R15] | Express many soft relational constraints with tractable MAP formulations. | A continuous truth assignment at a MAP solution is not a calibrated probability of a Boolean factory assertion. |
| Bayesian mixed or latent models [R12–R14] | Couple categorical, continuous, and count evidence. | Compatibility, identifiability, priors, and semantic scope still govern validity. |

For probabilistic circuits, tractability is relative to the circuit representation and query class. Efficient marginalization does not imply that arbitrary MAP queries, causal queries, or compilation from an arbitrary constrained factory model are equally efficient [R16, R17].

These are research options within the grounding, not competing definitions of OCBF. No inference family has been demonstrated here to recover calibrated factory-wide joint histories at the retained archive's scale.

<a id="s9"></a>
## 9. Robustness, identifiability, and uncertainty

### 9.1 Sources of uncertainty

| Uncertainty | Example | Suitable representation |
|---|---|---|
| Occurrence or state | Whether an Operation completed. | Alternative admissible histories. |
| Measurement | Timing noise or a location report's errors. | Observation likelihood. |
| Association | Which typed object or episode a report concerns. | Latent association or explicitly unresolved scope. |
| Coverage | Whether a sensor could have observed the event. | Observation opportunity, selection, and availability model. |
| Parameter or model | Unknown error rates or dependence structure. | Parameter prior, declared scenarios, or a model family. |
| Computation | Approximate BP or finite MCMC estimates. | Separate approximation or sampling qualification. |

A posterior probability describes uncertainty conditional on the model. It does not automatically include uncertainty about an omitted observation mechanism, a pruned candidate, or an incorrect semantic interpretation.

### 9.2 Configured trust and robust envelopes

When baseline trust is manually configured, $\theta_0$ defines an explicitly conditional posterior. Where several configurations are defensible, use a declared family $\Theta$ and define

$$
\underline P_\kappa(A)
=\inf_{\theta\in\Theta}P_{\kappa,\theta}(A),
\qquad
\overline P_\kappa(A)
=\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}P_{\kappa,\theta}(A).
\tag{18}
$$

This is a robust or credal envelope over the specified family [R18]. Shared source parameters, coupled constraints, and alternative dependence structures remain coupled within $\Theta$. Independently selecting every local worst-case parameter may produce a configuration outside that family.

A Bayesian alternative places a justified prior on parameters and integrates them out. That expresses different commitments from a set of admissible models. There is no need to average arbitrary scenarios into a purported posterior.

If only a finite selection of settings is evaluated, the observed range is a **scenario sensitivity range**. It is not an exhaustive bound over a larger $\Theta$, a posterior credible interval, or a probability that the true parameter lies in that range. Approximate inference also prevents calling numerical extrema certified bounds without further error control.

### 9.3 Identifiability and calibration

Observer-error models such as Dawid–Skene supply a conventional language for source confusion matrices [R20]. Their estimability depends on assumptions about observation overlap, conditional independence, model specification, and anchors or label orientation. Agreement can arise from copied observations or common mistakes.

Sparse or dependent factory evidence does not support an automatic claim that source reliability can be learned without ground truth. A manually assigned sensitivity or timing tolerance is an assumption; repeated agreement with a reconstruction produced from the same stream does not calibrate it. Reference conformity is also unsuitable as an unquestioned truth label.

Calibration is an empirical property of predictions evaluated against appropriate outcomes. This synthesis supplies no such validation. The relevant initial claim is interpretability under explicit assumptions, supplemented by sensitivity, rather than empirically certified confidence.

### 9.4 Failure and ignorance are different

A zero normalizing constant signals incompatible hard constraints or likelihoods. It is not a high-uncertainty posterior. Conversely, a broad posterior may be fully coherent.

An unmodeled proposition is outside the declared support. It should not receive an invented probability of 0.5. An in-model proposition with weak evidence may remain prior-dominated; that dependence should be visible. If a source cannot legitimately speak about work execution, its reported place cannot acquire that meaning merely through a smaller reliability parameter.

<a id="s10"></a>
## 10. Executions, variants, and conformance

### 10.1 Queries over histories

For a measurable query $q$ defined on the modeled history space, its posterior distribution is the pushforward

$$
P_{\kappa,\theta}^{q}(B)
=P_{\kappa,\theta}(q(L)\in B)
=\int \mathbf 1\{q(\ell)\in B\}\,
P_{\kappa,\theta}(d\ell).
\tag{19}
$$

This is the common mathematical interface for operation duration, relation existence, conformance cost, and exposure. Probabilistic databases offer a related possible-world interpretation of queries [R26]; provenance records how results depend on inputs, with probability evaluation as a separate task [R27].

The query definition includes the population, observation window, boundary handling, reference version, and units. Undefined values caused by an absent endpoint or an inapplicable reference need an explicit outcome or a stated conditioning event. They must not disappear silently from the denominator.

### 10.2 Process executions and dynamic variants

Let $\Xi_\rho(L)$ extract a process execution according to policy $\rho$, such as an anchored Job with relevant Operations and shared-resource events. Object-centric executions and variants depend on this policy [R5]. A shared Station can connect many jobs; the entire graph component is not necessarily an informative business case.

For a declared equivalence rule $\simeq_\rho$, define the variant $V_\rho(L)=[\Xi_\rho(L)]_{\simeq_\rho}$. Then

$$
P_{\kappa,\theta}(V_\rho=v)
=\int\mathbf 1\{V_\rho(\ell)=v\}\,
P_{\kappa,\theta}(d\ell).
\tag{20}
$$

A variant distribution can change with factory time because executions evolve, or with knowledge time because past associations and event times are revised. These changes should be distinguished. A change in extraction policy is a third, methodological change.

Directly-follows for object $o$ is derived from its participating events and their ordering in a world. With uncertain E2O links or times, it is a random relation. Equal timestamps or overlapping time bounds may induce a partial order; an arbitrary sorting tie-break must not be promoted into a known order.

Restricting a query to a Job or time window does not justify dropping relevant outside influences. Shared-resource dependencies and censored boundary events require marginalization, suitable boundary conditions, or a declared approximation.

### 10.3 Normative references

Let $\mathcal N$ specify applicable routing obligations, permitted ordering, multiplicity, and any declared duration requirements. Separate its provenance and version from $\mathcal S$ and from the evidence model.

For example, for a particular Operation $u$ whose start exists in the relevant scope, a declared completion deadline $\delta$ yields

$$
v_{u,\delta}(L;\mathcal N)
=\mathbf 1\left\{
\nexists e\in E:
\operatorname{Completes}(e,u)
\land \tau_e\in[\tau_{\mathrm{start}(u)},
\tau_{\mathrm{start}(u)}+\delta]
\right\}.
\tag{21}
$$

Here $\delta$ is a hypothetical reference parameter, not a measured Mammut standard. Assessing the obligation also requires an elapsed deadline and appropriate history scope. At an earlier factory horizon it is pending; missing later coverage can leave its outcome unresolved. A nonexistent or unestablished start is not silently treated as a satisfied instance of this obligation.

Object-centric behavioral constraints and alignments supply richer reference languages [R6, R7]. For an alignment-based query, define the cost as the minimum declared alignment cost over legal alignments of the realized execution to $\mathcal N$. Uncertain conformance evaluates this across realizations; alternatives without probabilities support bounds under their defined uncertainty model rather than invented probability weights [R8]. Fragment boundaries require special care [R9].

Using the same normative constraint to strongly favor conformity during reconstruction can hide the deviation subsequently measured. The primary descriptive posterior should therefore retain a documented separation from the reference being assessed. Reference-assisted reconstruction, if considered, is a separate conditional analysis.

### 10.4 State of the process versus state of the evidence

Two dimensions should remain separate:

- **Process status under a world and reference:** satisfied, violated, pending, or inapplicable.
- **Evidence assessment:** supported to a stated degree, unresolved, outside modeled support, or not assessable under the available coverage/reference.

A probability of violation is not a substitute for saying that the applicable routing reference is missing. Similarly, “unclassified wait” is a status of the evidence and model, not an additional physical cause.

### 10.5 What marginal beliefs can support

For a fixed finite set of requirements, linearity gives
$\mathbb E[\sum_j v_j]=\sum_j P(v_j=1)$ without independence. Assertion marginals can therefore support certain expected counts.

Joint uncertainty is needed for conjunctions, distributions of counts, interval overlaps, path criticality, rankings, and most whole-execution conformance. Independently thresholding or sampling marginals can construct histories that violate the model.

For events $A_1,\ldots,A_m$ with exact marginals, the Fréchet bounds are

$$
\max\left(0,\sum_{j=1}^{m}P(A_j)-(m-1)\right)
\le P\left(\bigcap_{j=1}^{m}A_j\right)
\le \min_j P(A_j).
\tag{22}
$$

These may be loose and omit useful structural information. Substituting approximate BP marginals without accounting for their error does not produce certified posterior bounds.

## 11. Attribution and business interpretation

### 11.1 Three different explanations

**Provenance explanation** identifies which reports, interpretations, and references contributed to a result. It answers how the result was derived [R27].

**Evidence influence** measures how a belief changes when evidence is removed or changed under a fixed model. For a query $q$, one possible measure is

$$
\Delta_g(q)
=\mathbb E_\theta[q(L)\mid D_\kappa]
-\mathbb E_\theta[q(L)\mid D_\kappa^{(-g)}].
\tag{23}
$$

Here $D_\kappa^{(-g)}$ is a specified deletion experiment. Removing an upstream observation may require removing its derived descendants, while retaining genuinely independent observations. Keep the candidate universe and model assumptions fixed for a pure evidence comparison; otherwise distinguish the effects. Nonlinear interactions make these influences non-additive.

**Causal attribution** concerns changes in factory outcomes under interventions. Neither provenance nor message magnitude nor $\Delta_g$ answers that question.

### 11.2 Descriptive duration exposure

For an explicitly defined candidate gap or waiting interval $G_j(L)$ and a condition $C_c(t,L)$, define

$$
Q_{jc}(L)
=\int_{t_0}^{t_1}
\mathbf 1\{t\in G_j(L)\}
\mathbf 1\{C_c(t,L)\}\,dt.
\tag{24}
$$

This measures time jointly in the interval and condition, conditional on both definitions. Examples include overlap with downstream occupancy or tracking loss. It does not establish that the condition caused the gap. If readiness is unknown, label the interval as a reconstructed gap rather than assuming productive waiting.

Different conditions can hold simultaneously. Their expected exposure minutes are generally non-additive. For an additive descriptive account, partition each world by exact combinations of condition states, including unresolved states where relevant. Mutually exclusive cells then sum to the interval's duration by construction, and their expectations also add. This is a partition of exposure, not a division of causal responsibility.

Missing coverage must remain distinguishable from observed absence of the condition. In particular, the earlier downstream_busy classification of a whole wait should not be reused as the measured overlap duration.

### 11.3 What a causal extension would require

A causal target might be

$$
\mathbb E[Y\mid \operatorname{do}(A=a)]
-\mathbb E[Y\mid \operatorname{do}(A=a')],
\tag{25}
$$

where $A$ is a well-defined intervention and $Y$ an outcome. Identification requires causal structure and assumptions about confounding, selection, mechanisms, and available observations; counterfactual attribution requires additional semantics [R28].

Temporal order and object-centric relations help formulate such a model but do not identify it. Capacity duals, critical paths, and observational associations answer other questions under their respective assumptions. This proposal does not claim recovered throughput, causal staffing productivity, financial value, or causal shares of delay from the current archive.

### 11.4 Executive-facing scientific meaning

The strongest supported direction is a common set of process questions whose answers retain:

- the Job/Operation cohort, factory period, and knowledge cutoff;
- the applicable reference and the exact meaning of the measured quantity;
- conditional support or a clearly labeled sensitivity range;
- observation coverage, unresolved associations, and provenance;
- the extent to which priorities change under reasonable trust or interpretation choices.

Examples are which job exceptions remain credible across trust assumptions, which apparent delays coincide with downstream occupancy, and which decisions are most sensitive to an unresolved association. These are interpretable screening questions. A formal value-of-information calculation additionally needs decision utilities and a model of future evidence; sensitivity alone should not be labeled economic value of information.

The benefit is semantic reuse: one corrected interpretation can change several process answers consistently. No particular screen layout or software workflow is prescribed here.

## 12. Formal properties and information-preserving reduction

The following properties belong to the proposed probability model, not automatically to an approximate implementation. Assume fixed $\mathcal S$, $\mathcal U$, and $\theta$, a proper prior, correctly interpreted evidence, and a finite positive evidence normalizer.

**Proposition 1 — Semantic support.** The posterior assigns probability zero to histories outside $\Omega_{\mathcal S,\mathcal U}$.

*Argument.* Its prior is supported on $\Omega$ and conditioning multiplies that measure by a nonnegative likelihood. This cannot create probability mass outside the prior support.

**Proposition 2 — Deterministic-copy invariance.** If $Y'=f(Y)$ is a known deterministic retransmission or transformation of an observed report, observing both supplies the same posterior as observing $Y$.

*Argument.* Conditional on $Y$, $Y'$ adds no information about $L$. Hence $P(L\mid Y,Y')=P(L\mid Y)$ for compatible observations. More generally, the same conclusion holds whenever $L\perp Y'\mid Y$. It does not cover an independently measured corroborating report.

**Proposition 3 — Neutral-evidence invariance.** An additional likelihood constant in $\ell$ leaves the posterior unchanged.

*Argument.* Its positive constant cancels from numerator and denominator. Silence is neutral only if its modeled likelihood has this property.

**Proposition 4 — Order invariance at a fixed evidence state.** If two arrival sequences produce the same interpreted evidence state $D_\kappa$, their exact posteriors agree.

*Argument.* Equation (7) depends on $D_\kappa$, not its arrival order. A revision must replace or reinterpret its affected likelihood; naively multiplying old and revised claims does not satisfy the premise. Changed candidate support or parameters also changes the premise.

**Proposition 5 — Likelihood-preserving reduction.** Suppose a summary $T(D)$ satisfies
$p_\theta(D\mid\ell)=c(D)\widetilde p_\theta(T(D)\mid\ell)$
for all admissible $\ell$, with $c(D)>0$ independent of $\ell$. Then conditioning on this summary likelihood yields the same posterior and query distributions.

*Argument.* The factor $c(D)$ cancels in Bayes' formula. Preservation across a family $\Theta$ requires the property for each configuration. This is the relevant sufficient-statistic condition; arbitrary averaging, maximum confidence, or fixed-frequency sampling need not satisfy it.

**Proposition 6 — Query consistency.** If two queries are related by $q_2=h\circ q_1$, their distributions obey $P^{q_2}=h_\#P^{q_1}$, where $\#$ denotes pushforward.

*Argument.* Both are projections of the same joint posterior. Separate heuristic scores need not satisfy this relationship.

These properties explain how a large message archive can, in principle, support a smaller inference problem without treating publication volume as evidence volume. The reduction must preserve the relevant likelihood or state its approximation.

A similar caution applies to representational refinement. Adding a second candidate label for the same physical event is not automatically harmless: it can multiply prior mass and alter event counts. Invariance under such refinements requires an explicit mapping and probability-preserving treatment of equivalent representations. A fixed skeleton alone does not supply that result.

### 14.1 Main research areas

| Research area | Question in this setting |

| Probabilistic object-centric event data | How should uncertain occurrence, association, attributes, and time form coherent OCEL-like realizations? |
| Temporal and artifact-centric process semantics | Which lifecycle obligations are meaningful over incomplete, evolving histories, and under what verification restrictions? [R6, R29] |

| Causal process reasoning | What additional data and assumptions would identify interventions, beyond descriptive exposure? |

State-bounded verification results in artifact-centric systems are useful because they show that formal guarantees depend on specific restrictions; they do not confer unrestricted decidability on an object-centric belief model [R29].

### 14.2 Recommended conceptual center

The recommended center is **a fixed object-centric skeleton with revisable, provenance-aware observation likelihoods and a joint history interpretation**. Bitemporal semantics belongs in the grounding from the outset. A full predictive temporal process model is optional: retrospective uncertainty does not require claiming a learned DBN, CTBN, or factory digital twin.

Manual trust should express source-by-assertion observation assumptions. Credible alternatives in trust, dependence, and association should support sensitivity analysis. Where the substrate supplies only marginals or a finite selection of coherent scenarios, the reported result should retain that limitation.

This center exploits the existing catalogue, structure adapter, source envelopes, episode anchors, and analytical vocabulary. It avoids requiring a separate fragile formula for every producer field: source interpretation carries the changing representation details, while process queries retain stable meanings. It does not eliminate semantic work. Unpredictable signals with unknown meaning cannot be unified correctly through probability alone.

### 14.3 Decision boundaries

| A coherent mathematical target for integrating heterogeneous evidence. | Empirical calibration or physical authentication of the archive. |
| Conditional operation, conformance, and exposure queries. | Independent measurements of labor, readiness, material shortage, or machine state absent supporting evidence. |
| Interpretable trust sensitivity and evidence tracing. | Causal loss shares or guaranteed recovered throughput. |
| Reuse of current structural assets and business vocabulary. | Factory-scale inference performance or a completed joint-query implementation. |

The scientific contribution proposed here is the integration and disciplined interpretation of these components. Its practical merit remains an empirical question. The [companion proposal](ocbf-grounding-proposal.md) states the lean version without prescribing software or delivery sequencing.
